You can't see what you're exposed to

A vault shows a clean number and a friendly name. It never shows the stack underneath: the markets it lends into, the collateral behind them, the single asset three layers down that other vaults lean on too. The same asset backs many vaults at once, so when it breaks, they break together. That's contagion, and it's invisible.

"When one asset wobbles, which vaults go down with it?"

Until now, answering that meant an hour of manual digging across a dozen disconnected tools, long after the damage was done.

Vaults, markets and assets

A vault is where money is deposited. A market is where that money is lent out. An asset is what ultimately backs it. Vaults lean on markets; markets lean on assets, and the same market or asset is shared by many vaults at once. That sharing is exactly how risk spreads.

A table can list any one vault's holdings. Only a graph shows that four vaults lean on the same asset at once, which is the whole point. So the map is force-directed: shared dependencies pull nodes together, and contagion becomes something you see rather than something you work out.

Click anything to see its connections

  • Hover a vault. See what it holds: the markets it allocates to, and the collateral behind them.
  • Hover an asset. See who's exposed to it: every market and vault that touches it, deduplicated into one node.
  • Hover a market. See both directions at once: the vaults using it, and the assets it leans on.

We show facts, not verdicts

We legally can't call a vault "safe" or "risky", a product that publishes that would be regulated investment advice. So I designed the opposite: no scores, no ratings, no judgment words.

Every point on screen is a sourceable fact, a 72-hour timelock, a fixed-price oracle, an 86% LLTV, marked as a strength or as something to review. The product shows you where the risks lie and lets you decide.

Toggle risk on, and every node carries a sourced breakdown: strengths, and the facts worth reviewing, in plain language.
